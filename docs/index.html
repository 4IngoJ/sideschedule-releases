How it works

It cheats the same way the Dock does.

Apple never gave regular apps a way to claim screen space like the Dock or the menu bar can. So SideSchedule fakes it: it keeps an eye on whatever window is in front, and the second that window tries to slide under the sidebar, it gets nudged back out. You’ll never notice it happening. I mostly don’t either, and I wrote the thing.

That trick needs one permission: Accessibility, under System Settings → Privacy & Security. It’s the only thing SideSchedule asks for besides Calendar access, and it’s the reason the app can do any of this at all.
